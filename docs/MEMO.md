# Memo: where to spend next quarter's retention budget

**To:** Head of Marketing and Finance. **Data:** transactions of a UK online gift-ware retailer, 2009-12-01 to 2011-11-30. Revenue is net of returns and is not profit (the data has no costs).

## The decision
Which customer groups should the December to February retention budget go to, and what happens to revenue if we do nothing?

## Recommendation
Contact the 1,941 customers in Champions, Loyal and At risk, high frequency (45% of identified customers, 86% of their revenue). Skip the other groups: their expected loss per customer is below average. Hold back a random 10% of the contact list so the effect is measured, not assumed.

| Contact these | Customers | Share of revenue | Did not buy last winter | Revenue at risk |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Champions | 951 | 66% | 34% | £441k |
| Loyal | 603 | 13% | 56% | £140k |
| At risk, high frequency | 387 | 8% | 65% | £99k |

## What we found
- **Revenue is flat; orders per customer is the weak spot.** Year 2 net revenue is +1.9% on year 1 (£9.16M to £9.33M). For customers with an ID it moved -£166k: active customers +£101k, orders per customer -£355k (-4.3%), basket value +£87k.
- **Customers come back unevenly.** 28% bought only once. Of each new cohort, about 21% buy the next month and 18% buy 12 months later.
- **A few customers carry the business.** The top 10% of customers hold 63% of revenue. Champions are 22% of customers and 66% of revenue, and 34% of them did not buy in the same quarter last winter.
- **Doing nothing is not free.** If nothing is done and customers behave as they did last winter, about £905k of next quarter's revenue (46% of today's quarterly run-rate) is at risk because those customers go quiet; 75% of that sits in the groups above. Expected revenue from today's identified customers is then about £1.33M.

## Expected impact: a scenario, not a result
| If the campaign lowers their lapse rate by | Revenue protected next quarter | Per customer contacted |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| 5% (assumed) | £26k to £34k | £13 to £18 |
| 10% (assumed) | £52k to £68k | £27 to £35 |
| 20% (assumed) | £104k to £136k | £54 to £70 |

The low end uses all-season average lapse rates, the high end last winter's. The lapse reductions of 5%, 10% and 20% are **assumptions**: nothing in this data measures a campaign. Judge the per-customer figure against what a contact costs and the margin on the sales it protects; we have neither.

## Assumptions and risks
- This winter's lapse rates match last winter's. The 95% interval on the Champions rate is 31% to 37%, but it is one winter, and if that winter was unusual so are the rates.
- Revenue at risk = a segment's quarterly run-rate (last 12 months / 4) times its lapse rate. Guest checkouts (13% of revenue) have no customer ID and are outside this analysis.
- Customers who would have bought anyway can absorb the budget; the hold-out is the safeguard. Many customers are wholesalers (per the dataset's description) and one account is 3.5% of all revenue, so a consumer-style offer may fit badly.
